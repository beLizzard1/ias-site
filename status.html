Where we are

Project status

Last updated 2026-08-22

A running snapshot of what's built, the findings that are steering the work, and what's in flight. Honest about the rough edges.

Perturbation core

Done

Embeddings-first shells with exact cosine geometry — unit(anchor + ε·tangent), cos = 1/√(1+ε²) — and reproducible, index-addressable sampling. Test suite green; the documented quickstart runs end to end.

Real anchor extraction

Done

Real 512-D anchors extract from photos via InsightFace / antelopev2, and a shell dataset builds and probes around a real identity locally (no GPU needed for the embedding dataset itself). Fixed a recognition-model-selection bug and pinned the model pack with a verified digest.

Face compositor

Done

Warps and blends a generated face into a real background photo, keeping the real hair and scene. A face-interior mask removes the doubled-hairline seam; a hair-aware pass re-lays strands that fall over the face.

Finding: the compositor itself is near-lossless — putting a face back into its own photo differs by at most one level per pixel.

Rendering (Arc2Face)

Works, with a caveat

Face generation from an embedding runs end to end. The limitation is fidelity: the model reconstructs facial structure from the identity vector but fills in hair, colour, and presentation from its own prior, so renders can look stylised and not match the person.

For the measurement this matters little — the edge model crops around facial landmarks, so background and hair realism are for human-facing figures, not for what the device sees.

Edge model (ESP-DL)

Fixed & verified; chip parity open

The “identical embeddings” bug is resolved. Differential testing against an independent int8 reference — tracing every node, each tensor in its own buffer — localised three defects, all in the vendor's generic C/C++ fallback path that only a host build exercises: a packed Conv filter layout the loader didn't unpack, a PReLU kernel skipping requantisation, and a memory planner aliasing every tensor to the same offset. Two are reported upstream (#319, #320); the #319 fix is submitted as PR #321, and the memory-planner defect was already fixed on esp-dl's own master.

The submodule has since been bumped to esp-dl v3.3.9 — which needed one more host-only fix, for pixel-conversion Kconfig symbols left undefined off-device — and results are unchanged: black/noise frames return no face, same-identity pairs score roughly 0.6–0.78 cosine, cross-identity pairs roughly ±0.16. That's agreement with an independent reference, not with the chip — the open question the on-device harness exists to close.

Refined acceptance surface

First surface measured

Per-direction boundary search on 20 rays around one real identity (Dr James’), bisected against the ESP-DL SiL to ±0.03 ε, three renders per test point (median score, majority verdict). 18 of 20 rays bounded inside the dataset; 2 right-censored at the dataset's cos 0.60 edge.

Boundary angle mean 32.7° ± 12.9°, range 6.8°–49.5°, anisotropy 7.2× — an isotropic discriminator would draw a flat circle; this INT8 model draws a star. These are the pilot's numbers, superseded by the 64-direction pass below; the figure to cite is 7.6×. See the Explorer for the plotted surface and the caveats (one identity, renderer noise inside the measurement, detector threshold relaxed to 0.3).

Higher-resolution refine pass

Done — 64 directions

All 64 directions are now bisected against the same anchor and SiL. Of the 64: 3 rays had their unperturbed render rejected by the detector and cannot carry a boundary, 7 are right-censored at the corpus edge (cos 0.60), and 3 resolved on too few evaluations to trust. The remaining 51 usable rays give a boundary angle of 37.7° ± 13.3°, range 6.8°–52.1° — an anisotropy ratio of 7.6×.

Two measurement bugs were caught and fixed on the way, and both had made the result look more dramatic than it is. Rays resolved on only two evaluations produced a spurious 30× ratio until a minimum-evaluation filter was added; and detection failures were initially plotted as scores of zero, which drew convincing but entirely fictitious “identity cliffs”. A score of zero means no face was found, not a face that scored badly, and the two are now drawn separately.

On-device harness

Started

The board now speaks the host protocol over its native USB, and host-versus-device parity has been measured rather than guessed. Feed both sides identical pixels and identical keypoints and their features agree to cosine 0.980–0.983 — close, but not bit-exact. Let each side run its own detector and that drops to 0.900, because the two detectors place landmarks differently: never identical, boxes up to 5 px apart, detection scores differing by 0.194 on average.

Most importantly, the thing that actually matters moved very little. Re-measuring boundaries on the device rather than the host displaced them by −1.1° ± 4.3° across 8 rays — within noise. So the host SiL is a good proxy for bulk surface mapping, and the device is the arbiter.

Detection could be lifted onto the host, which would raise agreement from 0.900 to 0.975. It will not be. The measurement target is the deployed pipeline — detect, crop, embed, decide — and improving the number by deleting the divergent component would be measuring a system nobody ships.

The threshold band

Measured

Any decision threshold between 0.269 and 0.602 gives zero false accepts and zero false rejects on this subject's nine photographs and 1,164 synthetic impostors. Every choice inside that 0.334-wide band reports identical FAR and FRR — and yet across it the acceptance region goes from “95% of directions still accepted at the edge of the corpus” to “a bounded star averaging 28.8°”.

That is the finding with the sharpest governance edge on this page. An operator tuning a threshold has the usual error rates to look at, and inside this band they are flat and perfect. Nothing in that view reveals that the shape of what the system accepts is changing enormously underneath.

How an identity is enrolled

Settled

Anchoring on the mean of several photographs rather than one beats a single image by +0.115 leave-one-out, and widens the margin over impostors from +0.462 to +0.623. The centroid stays. Synthetic identities therefore get several images each rather than the real subject being cut down to one — and it is worth saying plainly that real enrolment takes multiple angles anyway, mixed with liveness and robustness checks, so a single-image anchor was never the realistic case.

Do the renders look like the person?

Tested

The recognition model said the renders were more like the subject than her own photographs are like each other. The subject said she did not see herself in them. A forced-choice test with the reference photograph visible settles the direction: observers picked correctly on 16 of 16 trials targeting her, including an observer who had never met her.

So both statements survive, and stating them together is the finding: the renders sit above stranger-level identity signal and below felt likeness. It is a floor, not a ceiling — the distractors in that run sat around 0.08 cosine away, plainly different people. The version on this site is 2.6× harder, and because its per-trial distances are known it can measure human accuracy as a function of embedding distance.

Is the subject poorly represented?

Answered, with one open confound

A fair worry, given her anchor sits further from the synthetic pool than a synthetic anchor does. On the evidence, no. Her renders land at the 50th percentile of 20 comparison identities, so the generator reproduces her about as well as it reproduces anyone; and part of the distance gap is arithmetic, since averaging several photographs demonstrably pushes an anchor away from the pool.

The inference also runs the other way: distance from an impostor pool is what good separation looks like, not evidence of neglect. Underrepresentation would show as compressed genuine scores or impostors crowding the threshold, and neither is present. What cannot yet be settled with one real identity is whether real faces generally sit off a synthetic pool's manifold — that needs several real anchors, which the corpus does not have.

The through-line

  • The perturbation stays in identity space at known amplitudes — that's what makes a boundary crossing meaningful.
  • Realism is for communicating with humans; the device crops to the face, so the surface it maps is unaffected by the surrounding scene.
  • Rendering is done lazily on a chosen subset — never all points up front — and those renders double as the inputs that seed the search on the real device.
